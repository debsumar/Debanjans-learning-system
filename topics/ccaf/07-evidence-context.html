Evidence, synthesis, and context control

Keep claims traceable, preserve useful precision, and separate durable knowledge from task state.

Domain 5Context management and reliabilityWeight unverified
In one minuteSkills in scopeTraceable findingsSynthesisContext lossDurable artifactsMCQsQuick recall

In one minute

  • Store each claim with source, location, excerpt, and confidence; confidence does not identify origin.
  • Preserve claim-source mappings through synthesis; deduplication must not silently erase credible disagreement.
  • Extract exact numbers and dates before repeated summarization; a shorter context can still lose decisive precision.
  • Filter oversized tool output to fields needed for the current task; reduced payload is not a substitute for source validation.
  • Keep findings for evidence knowledge and manifests for task recovery state; durable files are related but not interchangeable.

Skills in scope

This provisional mapping remains subject to the official guide when it is published.

  • Preserve claim provenance with structured finding, source, location, excerpt, and confidence fields.
  • Preserve claim-source mappings and expose credible conflicts during synthesis.
  • Mitigate precision loss, lost-in-the-middle risk, and oversized tool output.
  • Use persisted facts, findings, scratchpads, and manifests without treating them as interchangeable.

Traceable findings

A finding records a claim with the evidence needed to inspect it later. Its provenance includes the source and a usable location such as document and page, while an excerpt shows relevant supporting text. Confidence is an assessed certainty signal; it cannot replace claim origin or location.

Structured records avoid a raw text blob whose claims cannot be traced after handoff. A schema can require fields, but valid structure alone does not establish that a source supports a claim or that a confidence value is calibrated.

ConfuseProvenance identifies claim origin and location through source, document, page, or excerpt. Confidence is a certainty signal about a finding. Provenance and confidence answer different questions; neither token substitutes for the other.
Common wrong turn

“A confidence value is enough evidence for a synthesized claim.” — Confidence can guide review, but a reviewer still needs source, location, and excerpt to trace what supports the claim.

Combine findings without losing origin

Synthesis combines evidence while retaining claim-source mappings. It may remove true duplicates, but two credible sources that disagree remain a conflict to attribute rather than an invitation to average, silently choose, or invent a reconciliation. Available publication or date context can help readers inspect the disagreement.

  1. Collect structured findings from each exploration scope.

    Keep each claim tied to source, location, excerpt, confidence, and any applicable tags or date information.

  2. Compare claims and retain their source mappings.

    Group duplicates only when they represent the same supported claim; do not merge away origin.

  3. Expose credible conflicts with attribution.

    State which sources support incompatible claims and preserve their evidence instead of asserting an unsupported resolution.

  4. Produce an attributed answer from the retained evidence.

    The answer can summarize the evidence while allowing a reviewer to trace claims back to their sources.

Flow: sources yield structured findings; synthesis retains mappings, handles duplicates and credible conflicts, then produces an attributed answer. This text is the compact equivalent of the diagram.

Common wrong turn

“Deduplication resolves credible disagreement.” — Deduplication removes repeated evidence for the same claim. Credible conflicting claims remain visible with their respective attribution.

Protect precision before context becomes bulky

Repeated summarization can generalize or omit precision facts such as dates, percentages, and numbers. Large inputs also create a lost-in-the-middle risk: relevant material can receive less attention when buried in a long prompt. Preserve exact facts outside the next summary, process large material in sections when appropriate, and inject only the relevant precision into later context.

External tools can return much more data than the current decision needs. Select required fields before passing a result onward; ordinary field filtering does not need another model call. This reduces context load, but it does not prove the selected fields are complete, accurate, or sufficient for a conclusion.

Common wrong turn

“A summary automatically keeps the numbers that matter.” — Treat exact dates, percentages, and other decision-critical values as precision facts to preserve and reinject explicitly.

Separate durable knowledge from recovery state

A findings artifact carries exploration knowledge for later review or synthesis. A task manifest records task identity and recovery state so interrupted work can be reconciled. Scratchpads can hold task-level investigation notes, while persisted facts hold selected information needed across iterative prompts.

Worked scenario: A text-adventure loop keeps selected case facts in a file. Each turn loads those facts, obtains the full world state, filters it to the current-room facts, and sends only that focused material to the next prompt. The selected facts remain prompt-relevant state; the full world state remains outside the prompt unless a task needs more.

  1. Explorer investigates noisy source within a narrow write boundary.

    The explorer may write only findings artifacts, including its scratchpad, and the task manifest. It never edits source files. Its scratchpad appends question, entry points, call chain, key files, evidence, and gaps; it never rewrites an earlier scratchpad section.

  2. Manifest records resumable task state.

    The explorer or coordinator updates task identity, status, and resume information in the manifest. After shutdown, error, or exhausted credits, the coordinator reads this focused state to resume recorded work; it cannot recover investigation that was never written.

  3. Synthesizer reads focused investigation artifacts.

    The synthesizer reads the append-only scratchpads and findings, not raw source, then writes a coherent synthesis and identifies unsupported gaps. It does not reinterpret a manifest as evidence.

  4. Coordinator routes from artifacts, not codebase noise.

    The coordinator consumes findings, scratchpads, synthesis, and manifest state for dispatch, recovery, and aggregation. Raw-source exploration stays with the explorer so coordinator context remains focused.

Here, findings preserve evidence knowledge, the manifest preserves recovery state, the scratchpad preserves append-only per-task investigation, and the synthesizer produces an integrated report. They are related durable artifacts with different roles and write permissions, not interchangeable files.

ConfuseFindings preserve evidence knowledge for synthesis; manifest preserves task and recovery state for orchestration. Findings and manifest can both be durable artifacts, but findings do not replace a manifest, and a manifest does not replace evidence findings.

MCQs

  1. A research record contains a claim and confidence score but cannot show where the claim came from. Which addition most directly fixes the gap?
    • A. A higher confidence threshold
    • B. Source, location, and supporting excerpt
    • C. A larger context window
    • D. A task status value
    Answer

    Answer: B — Source, location, and supporting excerpt.

    Reason: These fields preserve provenance so a reviewer can trace the claim to supporting evidence.

    • A — A higher threshold is useful when routing uncertain items for review, not when origin evidence is absent.
    • C — A larger context window is useful when more input must fit, not when a stored claim lacks attribution.
    • D — A task status value is useful for recovery state, not for tracing evidence.
  2. Two findings cite different publications and give incompatible values for the same measure. What should synthesis do?
    • A. Keep both attributed claims as a credible conflict
    • B. Average the values into one number
    • C. Keep the higher-confidence value and remove the other source
    • D. Deduplicate both because they discuss one measure
    Answer

    Answer: A — Keep both attributed claims as a credible conflict.

    Reason: Credible disagreement must remain inspectable with claim-source mappings rather than receive an invented resolution.

    • B — Averaging is appropriate only when a justified statistical method and compatible data support it, not for unexplained conflicting claims.
    • C — Selecting one source is appropriate only after an explicit evidence-based evaluation, not merely because another claim has a confidence signal.
    • D — Deduplication is appropriate for repeated equivalent evidence, not for incompatible values.
  3. A synthesis summary retains every claim but drops the links from claims to their original documents. What was lost?
    • A. Claim-source mappings
    • B. Tool-output filtering
    • C. Task recovery state
    • D. Loop termination control
    Answer

    Answer: A — Claim-source mappings.

    Reason: Without mappings, a reader cannot trace a synthesized claim back to its origin.

    • B — Tool-output filtering is appropriate when reducing bulky returned data, not when attribution disappears during synthesis.
    • C — Task recovery state belongs in a manifest, not in a missing source link.
    • D — Loop termination control is appropriate for agent execution state, not for evidence attribution.
  4. An iterative summary changes an exact regulatory percentage into vague wording. What is the best mitigation?
    • A. Preserve the exact percentage as a precision fact and reinject it later
    • B. Summarize the prior summary again
    • C. Include every raw document in every prompt
    • D. Replace the number with a confidence score
    Answer

    Answer: A — Preserve the exact percentage as a precision fact and reinject it later.

    Reason: Exact numbers, dates, and percentages should survive outside repeated condensation.

    • B — Another summary is appropriate only when it preserves needed facts, not when repeated condensation caused the loss.
    • C — Full raw material is appropriate for a focused review when needed, not as a default that increases context load.
    • D — Confidence is appropriate for uncertainty routing, not as a substitute for an exact value.
  5. A critical exception is buried midway through a long source. Which handling best addresses the stated risk?
    • A. Process the source in sections and retain the exception as a precision fact
    • B. Put the same whole source into a larger prompt without inspection
    • C. Keep only the opening and closing paragraphs
    • D. Convert the exception into task status
    Answer

    Answer: A — Process the source in sections and retain the exception as a precision fact.

    Reason: Section handling and explicit preservation mitigate loss of relevant material buried in a large context.

    • B — A larger prompt is appropriate only when capacity is the actual constraint, not as assurance that middle material is retained.
    • C — Opening and closing excerpts are appropriate for a short overview, not when known evidence sits in the middle.
    • D — Task status is appropriate for orchestration recovery, not for preserving source content.
  6. A search tool returns a large JSON response, but the next decision needs only an order number and current state. What should the workflow pass onward?
    • A. Only the required fields
    • B. The full response plus every prior tool result
    • C. A model-generated rewrite of every field
    • D. An empty result to avoid context use
    Answer

    Answer: A — Only the required fields.

    Reason: Field filtering reduces context burden while preserving data needed for the current decision.

    • B — Full output is appropriate when complete inspection is necessary, not when known required fields are sufficient.
    • C — A model rewrite is appropriate when interpretation is needed, not for ordinary deterministic field selection.
    • D — An empty result is appropriate only when no returned data matters, not when the decision needs two fields.
  7. A coordinator must restart after interruption and determine which tasks remain running or failed. Which artifact is primary?
    • A. Task manifest
    • B. Findings artifact
    • C. Source excerpt
    • D. Confidence field
    Answer

    Answer: A — Task manifest.

    Reason: The manifest holds task identity and recovery state used to reconcile and resume durable work.

    • B — A findings artifact is appropriate for evidence knowledge and synthesis, not for primary lifecycle recovery state.
    • C — A source excerpt is appropriate for claim support, not for task reconciliation.
    • D — A confidence field is appropriate for uncertainty assessment, not for identifying interrupted tasks.
  8. A synthesizer needs conclusions, entry points, key files, and a task question from several investigations without reopening raw source. Which artifact best fits each investigation?
    • A. Append-only scratchpad
    • B. Permission rule
    • C. Terminal response text
    • D. Tool-choice mode
    Answer

    Answer: A — Append-only scratchpad.

    Reason: A scratchpad preserves structured per-task investigation notes for later synthesis.

    • B — A permission rule is appropriate for authorizing tool or command actions, not for storing investigation findings.
    • C — Terminal text is appropriate for reader-facing completion, not as durable structured research input.
    • D — Tool-choice mode is appropriate for constraining tool invocation, not for evidence storage.
  9. An explorer stores claims and sources, while a manifest records task status. Why should the system keep both?
    • A. Findings support evidence synthesis and manifest supports recovery state
    • B. Findings permit shell execution and manifest permits source citation
    • C. Findings replace all prompt context and manifest validates semantics
    • D. Both are interchangeable copies for the same purpose
    Answer

    Answer: A — Findings support evidence synthesis and manifest supports recovery state.

    Reason: The artifacts are durable but serve distinct evidence and orchestration responsibilities.

    • B — Shell execution and citation controls belong to different tool and evidence boundaries, not to these artifact roles.
    • C — Prompt context and semantic validation need separate controls; neither role follows from these two artifacts alone.
    • D — Interchangeability is appropriate only for identical redundant copies, not for knowledge versus lifecycle state.

Quick recall

Attempt each question before expanding it.

What fields make a finding traceable rather than a bare claim?

Preserve the claim with source, usable location such as document and page, supporting excerpt, and confidence. Confidence supplements provenance; it does not replace source evidence.

Why is valid structured output not enough to establish a supported finding?

A schema can require a shape, but it cannot by itself prove that the cited source supports the claim or that confidence is calibrated. Review provenance and evidence separately.

What must synthesis retain to let a reviewer trace each conclusion?

Retain claim-source mappings: each synthesized claim remains connected to its source, location, excerpt, and other relevant evidence context.

How should an answer handle two credible sources that conflict?

Keep both claims attributed, make the conflict visible, and avoid inventing a reconciliation. Deduplicate only genuinely repeated equivalent evidence.

Which facts deserve explicit preservation before repeated summarization?

Preserve precision facts such as exact dates, percentages, numbers, and other information that could change a conclusion, then reinject them into later context as needed.

What two controls reduce large-context risk without claiming perfect recall?

Process suitable large material in sections to address lost-in-the-middle risk, and filter bulky tool output to prompt-relevant fields. Both reduce burden; neither guarantees correctness.

Contrast persisted facts, findings artifacts, scratchpads, and task manifests.

Persisted facts support iterative prompt context; findings artifacts preserve evidence knowledge; scratchpads preserve structured task investigation notes; manifests preserve task identity, lifecycle, and recovery state.

Why can a coordinator use findings without directly ingesting raw exploration context?

An explorer can place structured evidence in a durable findings artifact or scratchpad. The coordinator uses those focused artifacts for synthesis, routing, and recovery while keeping its context cleaner.

Trace a durable evidence flow from source through interruption recovery.

Collect structured findings with provenance, preserve claim-source mappings through synthesis, retain precision facts and reduced tool fields in context, and use manifests to reconcile task state while findings and scratchpads preserve knowledge.
